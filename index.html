Health Code Decoder

Type a word like asthma and see how it shows up across the vocabularies inside US health records. Or paste a code from a medical bill, lab report, or shot record and see what it stands for.

What is CARTOS, and why does this page exist?

CARTOS is the terminology service run by the US Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT (ONC). It gathers the code systems and value sets that certified US health IT has to use, such as SNOMED CT, LOINC, RxNorm, ICD-10-CM, and the CDC's CVX vaccine codes, into one place, with a terminology browser and a standards-based FHIR API. It is free and needs no account.

This page is a small independent hobby demo of what anyone can build on it: every answer you see is fetched live from CARTOS, straight from your browser, with no server in between. It exists to help curious people learn how health data is coded. It is not a clinical tool.

About this page

Where the answers come from. Every result is fetched live from CARTOS, the public FHIR terminology service run by ONC, part of the US Department of Health and Human Services. When you search, your query is sent from your browser directly to cartos.healthit.gov, where HealthIT.gov's privacy policy applies. This page has no server of its own, sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and stores nothing except a copy of the small vaccine list in your browser. Your search also appears in the page address after the # sign, so a bookmarked or shared link carries the words or code you typed. Do not type anything that identifies a person.

An educational demo, not a clinical or production tool. ONC describes the CARTOS API as intended for terminology discovery, design-time queries and validation, research, and periodic retrieval of terminology content, and says production clinical applications must not rely on it for runtime queries (Cartos User Guide, as of September 2, 2026). This page uses it the way ONC intends: one interactive lookup at a time, at the pace a person types, within the published rate limits. Do not use this page, or the CARTOS API behind it, inside a production clinical system or for patient care decisions. Organizations that need terminology at runtime should run their own terminology service.

Not medical advice. A code's official label is the name a computer system uses for it. It is not a diagnosis, a treatment recommendation, or an explanation of your care, and terms shown here may be out of date or incomplete. Talk to your clinician about anything on your records.

What it covers, and what it can't. Word searches go through the HL7 US Core value sets for conditions, medications, lab tests, and procedures, plus the CDC CVX list. A code that is missing here may still be valid; it may sit outside those lists or belong to a code set CARTOS does not carry, such as CPT, HCPCS, or NDC. A code is never "wrong" just because this page cannot find it.

Be gentle with a shared service. CARTOS allows about 2,000 requests per 5 minutes per address. This page waits between keystrokes, reuses answers it already has, shows at most 20 results at a time, and pauses when CARTOS says to slow down. Please do not script against it.

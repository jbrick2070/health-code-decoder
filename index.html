What is CARTOS, and why does this page exist?

CARTOS is ONC's terminology service. It gathers the code systems and value sets that certified US health IT has to use, such as SNOMED CT, LOINC, RxNorm, ICD-10-CM, and the CDC's CVX vaccine codes, into one place, with a terminology browser and a standards-based FHIR API. It is free and needs no account.

This page is a small hobby demo of what anyone can build on it: every answer you see is fetched live from CARTOS, straight from your browser, with no server in between.

Where this comes from. Every result is fetched live from CARTOS, the free public terminology service run by the US Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT. Nothing you type is stored anywhere except your own browser for this visit.

This is a terminology lookup, not medical interpretation. A code's official label is the name a computer system uses for it. It is not a diagnosis, a recommendation, or an explanation of your care. Ask your clinician about anything on your records.

What it covers. Searches go through the US Core value sets for conditions, medications, lab tests, and procedures, plus the CDC vaccine list. A code that is missing here may still be valid; it may simply be outside those lists or in a code set CARTOS does not carry, such as CPT, HCPCS, or NDC.

Each is used here under its steward's terms.

Be gentle. CARTOS is a shared service with a limit of about 2,000 requests per 5 minutes per address. This page waits between keystrokes, reuses answers it already has, and pauses when told to slow down.
